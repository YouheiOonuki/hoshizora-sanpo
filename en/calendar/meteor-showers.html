Rates are NAOJ’s estimate of the most meteors per hour at the best time, under a dark sky where the Milky Way is visible. Under city lights you see only a fraction. Dates marked JST are Japan dates (UTC+9); in the Americas and Europe the same event may fall on the previous day.

The three major showers

  • PerseidsPeaks every year in mid-August
  • GeminidsPeaks every year in mid-December
  • QuadrantidsPeaks every year in early January

Other showers

Orionids

Peaks in late October. The radiant is in Orion.

Leonids

Peaks in mid-November. The radiant is in Leo.

Lyrids

Peaks in late April. The radiant is in Lyra.

Eta Aquariids

Peaks in early May. The radiant is in Aquarius.

Southern Delta Aquariids

Peaks in late July to early August. The radiant is in Aquarius.

Southern Taurids

Peaks in early November. The radiant is in Taurus.

Northern Taurids

Peaks in mid-November. The radiant is in Taurus.

What is a meteor shower?

Comets and similar bodies leave trails of dust along their orbits. Earth passes through the same trail at the same time each year, so meteors are frequent then. The meteors seem to shoot out from one point, the radiant, and the shower is named after the constellation it lies in.
