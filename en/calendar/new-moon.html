What is a new moon?

A new moon is the moment the Moon is in the same direction as the Sun. Its unlit side faces Earth, and it rises and sets with the Sun, so you cannot see it at night.

Stargaze around the new moon

Moonlight is brighter than you might think: around the full moon, faint stars and the Milky Way almost disappear. The few days around a new moon have no moonlight, so they are the best nights for the Milky Way, faint constellations and meteors. “See the sky” opens 9 p.m. on that date at your place.
