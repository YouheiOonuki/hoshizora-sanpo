Quadrantid meteor showerWhen to watch, where to look and what time

The Quadrantids are one of the three major annual meteor showers, peaking every year in early January. Its peak is short, and the count changes a lot from year to year. It is named after Quadrans Muralis, a constellation no longer in use; the radiant lies near the border of Boötes and Draco. The radiant climbs after midnight, and the best time is before dawn.

Where should I look?

Meteors seem to shoot out from one point in the sky, the radiant. The Quadrantids radiant is near the border of Boötes and Draco. Meteors appear all over the sky, not only near the radiant, so take in as much sky as you can.

How to watch

  • Find a dark place with a wide view of the sky, away from city lights.
  • Give your eyes at least 15 minutes to adjust. A bright phone screen undoes that; the planetarium’s Red light helps.
  • Lie back on a mat or a reclining chair. Binoculars and telescopes show too little sky, so leave them at home.
  • A bright Moon cuts the count; check “Moon at peak” above.
  • Stay safe at night: watch for traffic, dress for the cold and keep children with an adult.

Where the meteors come from

A meteor shower happens when Earth runs into dust left along the path of a comet or similar body. The source of the Quadrantids is not settled yet; asteroid 2003 EH1 is the leading candidate. Other showers are on the meteor shower calendar.
