Perseid meteor showerWhen to watch, where to look and what time

The Perseids are one of the three major annual meteor showers, peaking every year in mid-August. The shower is reliable every year, and in the Northern Hemisphere it comes on mild summer nights. The radiant is already up in the evening and climbs toward dawn, so the best hours are from after midnight until the sky starts to brighten. From the Southern Hemisphere the radiant stays low or below the horizon, so fewer meteors are seen.

Where should I look?

Meteors seem to shoot out from one point in the sky, the radiant. The Perseids radiant is in Perseus. Meteors appear all over the sky, not only near the radiant, so take in as much sky as you can.

How to watch

  • Find a dark place with a wide view of the sky, away from city lights.
  • Give your eyes at least 15 minutes to adjust. A bright phone screen undoes that; the planetarium’s Red light helps.
  • Lie back on a mat or a reclining chair. Binoculars and telescopes show too little sky, so leave them at home.
  • A bright Moon cuts the count; check “Moon at peak” above.
  • Stay safe at night: watch for traffic, dress for the cold and keep children with an adult.

Where the meteors come from

A meteor shower happens when Earth runs into dust left along the path of a comet or similar body. The source of the Perseids is comet 109P/Swift–Tuttle. Other showers are on the meteor shower calendar.
